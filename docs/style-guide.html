M MOVIE INFO APPstyle guide

The design at a glance. Every section a product should define is here. If one reads thin, that is a gap to fill before building.

Brand decisions (guided, made for efficiency)
  • Light vs dark default — dark. Netflix and IMDb are both dark-surface products because dark lets poster art and photography carry the color; a light UI would compete with poster imagery instead of framing it. Dark-elevated cards (#17171C, #212127) sit on a near-black base (#0A0A0C).
  • Accent — Netflix red (#E50914) as primary, IMDb yellow (#F5C518) as a secondary rating/eyebrow accent. One strong accent (red) for primary actions keeps it confident and restrained like Netflix; borrowing IMDb's yellow only for ratings and labels gives the "rating and credibility" cue IMDb owns, without diluting the primary accent or drifting toward Bookmyshow's multi-color, promo-heavy look. Two accents, each with one job, is still one decision.
  • Type pairing — Bebas Neue for display/headings, Inter for body/UI. A tall condensed display face reads as cinematic marquee/poster typography (the Netflix and IMDb title-card register) without licensing their exact faces; Inter stays neutral and highly legible for synopses, cast lists, and forms, so editorial confidence up top never fights readability below.
Bookmyshow's ticket-booking energy (bright multi-color chips, dense promo tiles, light backgrounds) is deliberately avoided throughout, this is a poster-forward, editorial browsing experience, not a transactional booking flow.

Interaction
Hover lift

Hover, translateY -2px at 150ms. Poster cards lift and scale slightly more (-4px, 1.02x) to feel tactile like a browsable shelf. State changes ease at 250ms. That is the whole app baseline.

Micro-interactions only in an application, CSS transitions on hover, focus, and state, and everything here stops under prefers-reduced-motion. Scroll reveals, entrance choreography, and animation libraries are a website concern, documented per site build, not part of the app design.
